# MAZE-SCORE: neural-scale test of the deployed-$N$ score shape — preregistration

**Status:** DRAFT until the commit carrying the Hopper smoke receipt; FROZEN thereafter
(changes only via dated amendment).
**Environment:** GMU Hopper, one `3g.40gb` A100 MIG slice per array task, env
`/scratch/lwang44/envs/maxrl` (torch 2.6.0+cu124). CPU-side teacher logic is numpy.
**Code:** `curriculum_maxrl/maze_gpu/train.py` at the committed revision, unmodified;
metric computed by the frozen wave-2 analyzer path `fact_analyze.py::read_run`
(`cov_auc_delta`). No file in either is edited by this study.
**Relationship to E2c / GATE-DR:** none. Different machine, different question,
no shared artifacts.

## Question this answers

The strongest live criticism of the ICLR draft is that the score's *positive* evidence
exists only at 640 parameters (the fixed-pool Acrobot tournament). The 1.26M-parameter
maze factorial tested an *estimator* ordering (MaxRL vs GRPO), never the *score shape*.
So the paper currently supports "rollout-aware difficulty targeting helps at 640
parameters" and nothing larger.

**This study runs the Acrobot contrast at ~2,000x the parameter count:** does sampling
by the deployed-$N$ utility $u_N(p) = (1-(1-p)^N) - p$ beat its $N=2$ slice $p(1-p)$,
and uniform, on time-integrated coverage, with a 1.26M-parameter transformer at the
deployed $N=32$?

## Design

Three arms, estimator fixed to `maxrl` throughout (this is a score test, not an
estimator test):

| arm | `--teacher` | score |
|---|---|---|
| `un` | `frontier_un` | $u_{32}(p) = (1-(1-p)^{32}) - p$ |
| `learn` | `learnability` | $p(1-p)$ (the $N=2$ slice; SFL-style) |
| `unif` | `uniform` | none |

All three share the identical Beta posterior (decay 0.7), Thompson draw, 0.15 uniform
floor, and level set; the *only* difference between `un` and `learn` is the utility
function. Verified by reading `train.py` before freezing.

**10 fresh independent seed blocks: 20–29.** Waves 1 and 2 used blocks 0–11; these
have never been run. Each block shares one SFT warmstart across its three arms
(`seed{s}_sft_warmstart.pt`, generated by the block's first arm), so arms within a
block are paired on initialization, task pool, and evaluation stream.

Fixed protocol, identical to maze wave 2: `--steps 250 --eval-every 25 --lr 1e-4
--rollouts 32 --tasks-per-step 8 --sft-steps 600 --d-model 128 --n-layers 6`,
no hindsight. Independent unit = seed block (10 blocks). Arms within a block are
paired observations, never independent replicates.

## Endpoints

- **Primary:** paired (same block) `cov_auc_delta` contrast **`un` − `learn`**, where
  `cov_auc_delta` = mean pass@8 coverage over in-training evals (steps 25–250) minus
  the post-SFT initial value — the identical quantity and code path as the registered
  wave-2 primary.
- **Secondary (one test):** paired `un` − `unif` on the same metric.
- **Descriptive:** `learn` − `unif`; final-step `delta_cov8`; per-level bands.

## Decision rule (frozen)

Support for the primary requires **both**:
1. the 95% paired-bootstrap CI (10,000 resamples, seed 20260813) of the block-level
   mean `un` − `learn` excludes zero; **and**
2. the point estimate is at least **+.005** cov-AUC (SESOI, set at roughly a quarter
   of the wave-2 MaxRL−GRPO block-level effect of +.0195).

The CI is the inferential anchor by design: with 10 blocks the exact sign test cannot
reach $.05$ below 9/10, so sign counts are reported descriptively for every arm pair,
never as the criterion. This is a deliberate correction of the granularity problem
identified in the maze wave-2 analysis.

The secondary uses the same two-part rule and is reported with Holm correction across
{primary, secondary}.

## Outcome branches (both written before running)

- **Supported:** the paper gains a neural-scale positive for the score shape; the
  Evidence-section sentence stating the score's support is small-scale by design is
  replaced by a statement of the tested scale, and Contribution 2 cites both scales.
- **Not supported:** the draft's existing calibrated language stands *and strengthens* —
  the boundary statement becomes "the score-shape advantage observed at 640 parameters
  did not transfer to a 1.26M-parameter transformer under this protocol," reported in
  Limitations with the same prominence. No re-running with different settings, no
  post-hoc metric substitution, no seed extension.

Either way the result is reported. n=10 blocks; no claim of generality beyond this
task family and scale.
