# Acrobot score-exponent findings — 2026-08-15

Two preregistered campaigns completed tonight, both analyzed exactly once under
frozen rules. They answer the question `body_iclr.tex:523-526` explicitly left
open, and the answer is negative for the paper's central claim.

| campaign | where | cells | analyzer |
|---|---|---|---|
| U64 over-shooting arm | Hopper, Xeon Gold 6240R, jobs 9375605 + 9375630 | 80/80 | `acrobot_u64/ACROBOT_U64_ANALYSIS.json` |
| Score-exponent dose–response | local, Core Ultra 7 265K | 160/160 | `acrobot_nsweep/ACROBOT_NSWEEP_ANALYSIS.json` |

## 1. The headline: peak-location specificity is not supported

The deployed estimator was pinned at **N = 16** in every arm of both campaigns.
Only the sampling score's exponent varied. If the paper's claim were right,
performance should peak when the score's peak sits where the deployed N puts it
(p\*₁₆ = .169).

It does not. The dose–response curve rises essentially monotonically **past** 16:

| arm | mean AUC | vs u16 |
|---|---|---|
| uniform | .65819 | −.01684 |
| u2 = p(1−p) | .64428 | −.03074 |
| u4 | .66815 | −.00688 |
| u8 | .66495 | −.01008 |
| **u16 (deployed N)** | **.67503** | — |
| u32 | .67931 | +.00428 |
| **u64 (argmax)** | **.68782** | **+.01279** |
| u128 | .68096 | +.00594 |

**argmax = u64, not u16. Spearman(exponent, mean) = +0.929.**

The over-shoot test agrees across both platforms:

| u16 − u64 | mean | 95% CI | exact p | pairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hopper (Xeon 6240R) | −.01127 | [−.02973, +.00572] | .2349 | 10/20 |
| local (Ultra 7 265K) | −.01279 | [−.02841, +.00241] | .1307 | 7/20 |

Neither is significant, both point the same way, and the point estimate favours
the **over-shooting** arm in both. A score peaked at p\* = .064 does at least as
well as one peaked at the "matched" .169.

**Conclusion: at this operating point the deployed-N peak location does not do
the work. Harder-peaked scores are simply better, up to somewhere around N ≈ 64.**

## 2. What survives, and it is not nothing

Shape dominance over the learnability slice is strong and now replicated on
three platforms, using the same logical seeds 20000–20019:

| u16 − p(1−p) | platform | mean | 95% CI | exact p | pairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2 (frozen) | macOS arm64 | +.04803 | [+.02094, +.07385] | .003361 | 15/20 |
| U64 campaign | Linux Xeon x86_64 | +.03217 | [+.01983, +.04491] | .000105 | 17/20 |
| dose–response | Linux Ultra 7 x86_64 | +.03074 | [+.01664, +.04534] | .000507 | 16/20 |

This is the first time the paper's Acrobot primary has been reproduced anywhere
outside the machine that produced it, and both new runs give a **tighter p-value
than V2's own**.

## 3. Three honest caveats, stated because they change what may be claimed

**(a) The two new campaigns are not independent evidence.** They use the same
logical seeds and the same deterministic engine, so close agreement between them
(−.01127 vs −.01279) is a *portability* check, not a doubling of sample size.
The 20 paired seeds are 20 paired seeds, not 40.

**(b) Effect magnitude is platform-sensitive.** On identical seeds the u16−p(1−p)
effect is +.048 on macOS arm64 but +.031/+.032 on both x86_64 hosts — roughly a
35% attenuation. Sign, significance and direction are robust; the magnitude is
not. Any number quoted in the paper should be attributed to its platform, and
the two x86 results agreeing closely while arm64 differs suggests the difference
is floating-point/architecture rather than noise.

**(c) The grid cannot resolve a maximum finer than its spacing.** u4 (.66815)
slightly exceeds u8 (.66495), which is within noise, and the fall from u64 to
u128 (.68782 → .68096) is small. The defensible statement is "increasing to
around N ≈ 64, then flattening or turning over", not a precise optimum.

## 4. What the paper must now say

The claim that must be dropped: *the score should peak where the deployed N puts
it*. That was the only advance over the ProCuRL / SFL / LILO p(1−p) literature,
and it has now been tested directly and did not hold.

The claim that is supported: **the deployed-N score shape dominates its N = 2
slice across every environment and estimator tested** — which is exactly the
reframe `RESEARCH_PLAN_2026-08-15.md` §2.1 already recommended on independent
grounds, now with a measured boundary attached instead of a silent confound.

The theory is untouched. The exact identity A_N(p) = 2(pass@N − pass@1) under
the stated convention, the truncation result T = N−1, and the closed-form
Beta-posterior priority all stand. What fails is a claim about *where the
resulting score should peak*, not about the algebra that produces it.

Practical consequences:

1. Risk-register item 2 ("peak-hardness confound named by a reviewer and the
   paper has no answer") is closed **by evidence**. The answer is now: we tested
   it, and peak location is not what matters.
2. `body_iclr.tex:523-526` should change from a concession that no arm tests
   peak-location specificity to a statement that an arm now does, with the null
   result reported.
3. A reviewer's strongest attack — "a hand-set difficulty target of ≈.17
   reproduces every number in your paper" — is now partly conceded and partly
   answered: a hand-set target does reproduce the results, but the score still
   beats p(1−p) robustly and is derived rather than tuned.
4. Nothing here bears on MAZE-SCORE, which probes scale (1.26M parameters) and
   is complementary. Under the reframed claim it remains the highest-value
   pending experiment.
