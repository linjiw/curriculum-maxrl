\begin{abstract}
Curriculum methods for RL with verifiable rewards summarize each task unit by a
pass rate $p$ and apply a learnability curve $f(p)$. That presumes the unit the
curriculum scores is the random object the group estimator consumes. It need
not be: a unit of uniformly uncertain tasks and a unit mixing mastered with
impossible tasks share a mean pass rate, yet only the first yields
mixed-outcome groups and non-zero updates. We formalize the mismatch. For a
permutation-equivariant binary group estimator, activity is a functional of the
\emph{success-count law}, $\mathcal A(z)=\sum_kP(K{=}k\mid z)\,M_{\mathcal
E}(k)$, with closed-form $M_{\mathcal E}$ for MaxRL, RLOO and GRPO; for
practical MaxRL it is $2\{\Pr(K{>}0)-\mathbb E[K]/N\}$ under \emph{any} joint
law, and the familiar $2(\passat{N}-\passat{1})$ is only its
conditionally-i.i.d.\ atomic slice, which factors as $p(1-p)\cdot w_{N-1}(p)$
and recovers $p(1-p)$ at $N{=}2$. For a coarse unit, plugging in the mean pass
rate over-predicts activity by exactly twice its excess all-fail probability.
In a preregistered 48-block neural-maze intervention, replacing that plug-in
with the shared-posterior count-law functional improves cov-AUC by $+.00666$
(95\% CI $[+.00441,+.00887]$), confirming causal relevance on that substrate.
Where the scored unit is the estimator's own, the deployed-$N$ shape beats
$p(1-p)$ by $+.0480$ and reproduces on two further platforms using the same
seeds. Three preregistered follow-ups bound it: performance rises \emph{past}
the deployed $N$, the score is starved as a standalone replay priority, and on
a level aggregating heterogeneous tasks the original contrast reverses
($-.0032$). The estimator defines the coefficient map; the curriculum defines
the unit over which that map is averaged, and these operations do not commute.
Coefficient activity is a principled, estimator-conditioned source of
curriculum hypotheses, not a measure of learning utility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

RL with verifiable rewards converts automatically checkable outcomes into
policy updates, and every update first pays for a group of $N$ model rollouts.
Curricula make that budget productive by summarizing each task unit with a pass
rate $p$ and sampling by a learnability curve $f(p)$ \citep{procurl,sfl}. That
presumes the unit the curriculum scores is the random object the estimator
consumes. Consider two units, each with mean pass rate $\tfrac12$ at $N{=}16$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:counterex}). In the first, every task is solved about half the
time: groups come back mixed and the estimator forms success-versus-failure
contrast on nearly every one. In the second, half the tasks are mastered and
half are impossible: every group is unanimous, all-pass groups carry no
contrast, all-fail groups are dropped, and realized activity is exactly zero.
Any $f(\bar p)$ scores them identically; the estimator does not. The question
is not which function of pass rate to use, but \emph{when pass rate is a
sufficient statistic at all}.

We answer for practical MaxRL. Its expected absolute coefficient mass obeys
$A_N=2(\Pr(K{>}0)-\mathbb E[K]/N)$ for \emph{any} group law
(Prop.~\ref{prop:mass}); under conditionally i.i.d.\ rollouts this is
$2\{1-(1-p)^N-p\}=2(\passat{N}-\passat{1})$ and factors as
\[
u_N(p)=A_N(p)/2=p(1-p)\cdot w_{N-1}(p),
\]
where $w_T(p)=\sum_{k=1}^{T}(1-p)^{k-1}$ is MaxRL's own weight function
(Cor.~\ref{cor:factor}). \textbf{Coefficient activity is learnability
reweighted by the deployed estimator.} $p(1-p)$ is the $N{=}2$ slice, and the
peak $p^\star_N=1-N^{-1/(N-1)}\simeq\log N/N$ is derived, not tuned. MaxRL
asked which \emph{objective} more compute should buy; we ask which
\emph{tasks} that objective should be fed.

Our contributions are organized by what each is entitled to claim.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theory, proved and machine-verified.} Activity is a functional of
the \emph{success-count law}, not of mean pass rate: $\mathcal
A_{\mathcal E,N}(z)=\sum_kP(K{=}k\mid z)M_{\mathcal E}(k)$, with closed-form
$M_{\mathcal E}$ for MaxRL, RLOO and GRPO. The familiar $p$-curves are its
conditional-i.i.d.\ slice; Cor.~\ref{cor:gran} prices a coarse scored unit
exactly; the bridge $\mathbb E[\hat g\mid x]=u_N(p)(\mu_+-\mu_-)$ is why a
coefficient surrogate is defensible; Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc} fixes the deployed
truncation at $T=N-1$. All checked as code contracts against the deployed
estimators.
\item \textbf{Registered and confirmed.} In a fixed Acrobot pool at deployed
$N{=}16$, $u_{16}$ beats $p(1-p)$ by $+.048$ under a frozen rule and
reproduces on two further platforms using the same seeds
(\S\ref{sec:acrobot}).
\item \textbf{Registered boundaries, reported as first-class results.}
Sweeping only the score exponent, performance rises \emph{past} the deployed
$N$ --- the harder-peaked shape helps, its peak location does not
(\S\ref{sec:peaktest}). As a standalone replay priority on AMaze the score is
starved: one Bernoulli per level visit against a critic read every timestep
(\S\ref{sec:amaze}). And where the curriculum scores a \emph{level}
aggregating heterogeneous tasks, the contrast reverses, $-.0032$ with CI
$[-.0054,-.0011]$ and effects at or above the registered SESOI ruled out ---
a sign consistent with Cor.~\ref{cor:gran}, which predicts the
\emph{calibration} bias and not the endpoint (\S\ref{sec:mazescore}).
Granularity,
not scale, separates these studies.
\item \textbf{Preregistered correction, confirmed.} On the same MAZE-SCORE
substrate, replacing the mean-pass-rate plug-in by the shared-posterior
count-law functional improves cov-AUC by $+.00666$ (95\% CI
$[+.00441,+.00887]$; \S\ref{sec:mazescore}). This establishes causal relevance
of the correction there, not that Cor.~\ref{cor:gran} predicted the learning
sign or that either arm beats $p(1-p)$. The LLM-scale interaction and Countdown
mechanism remain open and appendix-only.
\end{enumerate}
Throughout, curricula are evaluated with the estimator they ship.

\section{Coefficient activity under practical MaxRL}
\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{Setup.}
For a task $x$ let $\mathbf r\in\{0,1\}^N$ be the group's rollout rewards,
drawn from a joint law $Q_x$, and $K=\sum_i r_i$. Write $\bar
p=\mathbb E_{Q_x}[K]/N$ and $q_N=\Pr_{Q_x}(K>0)$; when the $N$ rollouts are
conditionally i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(p)$ these are $p$ and $\passat{N}=1-(1-p)^N$.
Let
$s_i=\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_i\mid x)$ be the response score and
$\hat g=\sum_iw_i s_i$ the group update. Then
$\passat{N}=1-(1-p)^N$. We analyze the zero-stabilizer idealization of the
released practical centered MaxRL convention, which assigns
\[
w_i=\frac{r_i}{K}-\frac1N\quad\text{when }K>0,
\qquad w_i=0\quad\text{when }K=0.
\]
All-pass groups also have zero contrast. The released implementation uses a
finite denominator stabilizer $\varepsilon$, multiplying the ideal
coefficients by $K/(K+N\varepsilon)$ when $K>0$; the identities below are
exact for the zero-$\varepsilon$ convention, not for its finite-$\varepsilon$
magnitude.

\begin{definition}[Finite-group activity geometry]
\label{def:geom}
A permutation-equivariant binary group estimator $\mathcal E$ maps a group
outcome to coefficients whose absolute mass, under binary rewards, depends only
on the success count: write $M_{\mathcal E}(k)=\|\mathbf
c_{\mathcal E}(k)\|_1$. Its \emph{activity} on a unit $z$ whose groups follow
the count law $P(K\mid z)$ is
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathcal E,N}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}P(K{=}k\mid z)\,M_{\mathcal E}(k),
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{MaxRL}}(k)&=2(1-k/N)\mathbf 1\{k>0\},\\
M_{\text{RLOO}}(k)&=\tfrac{2k(N-k)}{N(N-1)},\\
M_{\text{GRPO}}(k)&=\tfrac{2}{N}\sqrt{\tfrac{N-1}{N}}\sqrt{k(N-k)},
\end{aligned}
\]
the last for the sample-SD convention at zero stabilizer.
\end{definition}
\noindent
\textbf{The sufficient statistic for estimator activity is the success-count
law, not the mean pass rate.} Under conditionally i.i.d.\ rollouts
$P(K\mid z)$ is Binomial$(N,p)$ and the sum collapses to a Bernstein polynomial
in $p$ --- the familiar one-dimensional curves --- but that collapse is an
assumption about the scored unit, not a property of the estimator. All three
masses above are verified against the deployed implementations for
$N\in\{4,8,16,32\}$ and every $k$ (App.~\ref{app:proofs}).
Choosing an estimator fixes $\{a_{\mathcal E,k}\}$ and therefore which tasks can
emit an update at all, orthogonally to the objective-side question of which
finite-rollout objective to deploy \citep{maxrl,rl2ml}. What singles out
practical MaxRL is that its mass is affine in the two statistics $(q_N,\bar
p)$, so the sum collapses without any independence assumption.

\paragraph{Why $\ell_1$ coefficient mass.}
Three properties, and one limit. On a live MaxRL group the coefficients sum to
zero, so $\tfrac12\|\mathbf w\|_1$ is simultaneously the total positive and the
total negative mass: it measures how much success-versus-failure contrast the
estimator can form. If rollout scores are bounded, $\|s_i\|\le B$, then
$\|\sum_i w_is_i\|\le B\|\mathbf w\|_1$, so activity is a worst-case update
envelope that depends on the estimator alone and not on unknown score geometry.
And it is knowable before any rollout is spent, from a posterior over outcomes.
The limit is equally sharp: activity is not the gradient norm and not expected
improvement, because the realized update also carries the direction
$\mu_+-\mu_-$.

\begin{lemma}[Practical truncation order]
\label{lem:trunc}
The expectation of the drop-$K=0$ practical estimator equals the gradient of
the $(N-1)$-term truncated maximum-likelihood objective, not the $N$-term
objective.
\end{lemma}
\noindent
For $J_T(p)=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}(1-p)^t/t$, direct conditioning gives
\[
\mathbb E\hat g
=\left(\frac{1-(1-p)^N}{p}-(1-p)^{N-1}\right)\nabla p
=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}(1-p)^j\right)\nabla p
=\nabla J_{N-1}.
\]
The $-(1-p)^{N-1}$ term is the price of an \emph{outcome-dependent} baseline:
the control variate is subtracted only on groups that happened to contain a
success, so it is not mean-zero over the outcome law, and the shortfall is
exactly the probability that a designated rollout is the group's lone success.
The correction matters whenever a theoretical truncation parameter is mapped
to a deployed group size. \emph{This $N-1$ statement does not revise MaxRL's
theorem for its uncentered conditional estimator}; it characterizes the
released centered convention after its all-fail group is zeroed, so the shift
is a property of the deployed rule, not of the maximum-likelihood expansion.
Details and the non-dropped control-variate convention are in
App.~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Exact coefficient mass under any group law]
\label{prop:mass}
For \emph{any} joint binary group law $Q$ --- no independence and no identical
distribution required --- the practical estimator's expected absolute
coefficient mass is
\[
A_N(Q)=\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}|w_i|\right]=2\left(q_N-\bar p\right).
\]
If the rollouts are conditionally i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(p)$ this is
\[
A_N(p)=2\{1-p-(1-p)^N\}=2(\passat{N}-\passat{1}).
\]
\end{proposition}
\noindent
We call $A_N$ \emph{coefficient activity} throughout; readers coming from the
GRPO literature will know the same object as expected advantage mass. The
realized mass of a group is the deterministic function
$M(K)=2(1-K/N)\mathbf1\{K>0\}$ --- successes and failures each contribute
$(N-K)/N$ when $0<K<N$ --- so $A_N(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[M(K)]=2(q_N-\bar p)$ needs no
distributional assumption at all; only the scalarization to $p$ does. This
identity concerns coefficients, not gradient norm, signal-to-noise ratio, or
expected improvement: score-gradient geometry can change its downstream
effect.

The bridge to the expected update is exact: with
$\mu_\pm=\mathbb E[s_i\mid r_i{=}1/0,x]$ we have
$\mathbb E[\hat g\mid x]=u_N(p)(\mu_+-\mu_-)$ for $u_N=A_N/2$, so $u_N$ is the
estimator-controlled scalar factor in the expected update and the conditional
score geometry stays outside the mass calculation. Under i.i.d.\ rollouts
$u_N(p)=\Pr(r_1{=}0,\,K>0)=(1-p)\{1-(1-p)^{N-1}\}$: activity is the
probability that one rollout fails while a peer in the purchased group
succeeds. Both dead zones follow --- an all-fail task supplies no positive
contrast, an all-success task no negative contrast --- as does why $N$ belongs
in the curriculum's definition rather than its implementation.

\begin{lemma}[Per-member relabeling is coefficient-degenerate]
\label{lem:relabel}
For the centered MaxRL, RLOO and GRPO estimators in Def.~\ref{def:geom},
relabeling every member of an all-fail group to its own achieved goal changes
$K=0$ to $K'=N$ but leaves realized coefficient activity exactly zero.
\end{lemma}
\noindent
Thus hindsight creates a live contrast only if one goal is applied consistently
across the group and yields $1\le K'<N$; this is necessary for activity, not
evidence of learning utility. The two-line proof is in App.~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{corollary}[Activity is reweighted learnability]
\label{cor:factor}
Write MaxRL's population gradient as $\nabla J_T=\mathbb E_x[w_T(p)\nabla p]$
with weight function $w_T(p)=\sum_{k=1}^{T}(1-p)^{k-1}$ \citep{maxrl}. Then,
exactly,
\[
u_N(p)\;=\;p(1-p)\cdot w_{N-1}(p).
\]
\end{corollary}
\noindent
By Lemma~\ref{lem:trunc} the deployed estimator targets $T=N-1$, and
$p(1-p)\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(1-p)^{k-1}=(1-p)\{1-(1-p)^{N-1}\}=u_N(p)$. So
coefficient activity is the learnability score reweighted by MaxRL's own
weight function at the truncation the deployed estimator targets: $p(1-p)$
peaks at $\tfrac12$ regardless of estimator, $w_{N-1}$ decreases in $p$ and
grows with $N$ as $p\to0$, and their product peaks at
$p^\star_N=1-N^{-1/(N-1)}$, where an informative rollout meets an estimator
that still cares. The familiar binary-success score $p(1-p)$ is both RLOO's
i.i.d.\ expected half-mass and practical MaxRL's $N{=}2$ slice. SFL's
finite-group score is the corresponding realized RLOO mass up to a fixed
factor; for practical MaxRL at arbitrary $N$, the estimator-matched curve is
$u_N$.

\begin{corollary}[Task granularity]
\label{cor:gran}
Suppose a curriculum scores an aggregate $z$ (a level, template, or domain)
whose concrete tasks are $X\sim\nu(\cdot\mid z)$ with conditionally i.i.d.\
rollouts given $X$. Because $u_N''(p)=-N(N-1)(1-p)^{N-2}<0$, plugging the
aggregate's mean pass rate $\bar p_z$ into $u_N$ over-predicts its activity,
and the gap is exactly the aggregate's excess all-fail probability:
\[
A_N(\bar p_z)-2\,\mathbb E_X[u_N(p_X)]
=2\left[\Pr(K{=}0\mid z)-(1-\bar p_z)^N\right]\;\ge\;0 .
\]
\end{corollary}
\noindent
Both sides follow from Prop.~\ref{prop:mass} applied to the aggregate's own
group law, whose $q_N$ is $1-\Pr(K{=}0\mid z)$. Curvature says who pays: near $p^\star_N$ the penalty is
$\approx\tfrac12|u_N''|\operatorname{Var}(p_X)$ with
$|u_N''(p^\star_N)|=(N-1)/(1-p^\star_N)\approx34.7$ at $N{=}32$, against the
exact and far milder $u_2(\bar p)-\mathbb E[u_2(p_X)]=\operatorname{Var}(p_X)$.
\textbf{Raising $N$ moves activity toward harder tasks and, near that active
region, amplifies its sensitivity to how coarsely the curriculum names a task}
(\S\ref{sec:mazescore}). This is a local statement: for a fixed heterogeneous
$\nu(\cdot\mid z)$ with all $p_X>0$, both $\mathbb E[(1-p_X)^N]$ and
$(1-\bar p_z)^N$ vanish as $N\to\infty$, so the gap is not monotone in $N$
globally.



\paragraph{Instantiation.}
\label{sec:method}
FrontierMax operationalizes the geometry without changing the within-group
estimator: each task keeps discounted Beta evidence, draws a posterior pass
rate $\tilde p_x$, and is sampled with probability $q(x)=\epsilon/M+
(1-\epsilon)[u_N(\tilde p_x)]^\gamma/\sum_j[u_N(\tilde p_j)]^\gamma$. The floor
$\epsilon$, concentration $\gamma$, decay and posterior estimator remain
empirical choices; the theory supplies the utility and its $N$-dependence, not
a parameter-free scheduler. An exact deterministic posterior priority is also
available (App.~\ref{app:proofs}), which the AMaze arm uses; scoring the
posterior \emph{mean} instead overstates activity by a closed-form uncertainty
penalty --- the same concavity that drives Cor.~\ref{cor:gran}. Coefficient
activity is the shared quantity, not a license to conflate a proved identity, a
tested fixed-pool teacher and an untested replay overlay;
Table~\ref{tab:instantiations} in App.~\ref{app:algs} attaches a status to each
object separately.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Count-law curriculum step (fixed group size $N$)}
\label{alg:countlaw}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE estimator $\mathcal E$, units $\mathcal Z$, decay $d$, floor $\epsilon$, concentration $\gamma$
\STATE Write $X_z=(W_z,Z_z,S_z,S_{2,z})$ and initialize $X_z\leftarrow X_0$ from an i.i.d.-Binomial prior
\STATE Retain the full count-bin posterior only for GRPO
\STATE On observing count $k$: $X_z\leftarrow X_0+d(X_z-X_0)+(1,\mathbf1\{k=0\},k,k^2)$
\STATE MaxRL: $a_z\leftarrow2(1-Z_z/W_z-S_z/(NW_z))$
\STATE RLOO: $a_z\leftarrow2(NS_z-S_{2,z})/[W_zN(N-1)]$
\STATE GRPO: $a_z\leftarrow\sum_k\widehat P_z(K=k)M_{\mathcal E}(k)$
\STATE $v_z\leftarrow\max(a_z,0)^\gamma$; use uniform weights if $\sum_jv_j=0$
\STATE $q(z)\leftarrow\epsilon/|\mathcal Z|+(1-\epsilon)v_z/\sum_jv_j$; sample $z\sim q$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

Corollary~\ref{cor:gran} organizes what follows, and the organizing variable
is not scale. The $p$-only reduction is exact when the curriculum's scored unit
is the unit over which rollouts are i.i.d., and lossy otherwise. The CPU chain
and Acrobot satisfy that condition, and the deployed-$N$ shape beats $p(1-p)$
in both. An exact-probability Digits factorial is instead a boundary: its
estimator-by-sampler interaction is unsupported and both matched samplers fall
below uniform (App.~\ref{app:protocols}); it is not a replication. MAZE-SCORE
violates the unit condition, drawing a single maze per group
while the posterior pools over a level's many mazes, and it is the one study
where the shape loses. We read this \emph{post hoc}, across studies not
designed to test it. P0 then varied only the plug-in versus count-law
functional and confirmed a learning gain on the same coarse-unit substrate
(\S\ref{sec:mazescore}). Figure~\ref{fig:claimmap} states for each contrast
what it is and is not: frozen primaries, supporting measurements, three
preregistered boundaries, and the prospective correction.



\subsection{Fresh fixed-pool Acrobot score test}
\label{sec:acrobot}

We isolate the arbitrary-$N$ score on official Gymnasium
\texttt{Acrobot-v1}, where the scored unit \emph{is} the task. Eight nested
tip-height predicates share one task-blind H64 actor (640 parameters), the
hardest being exactly native Acrobot success. All arms use practical MaxRL at
$N=16$, no hindsight, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, and a nominal two
million paid transitions; twenty fresh paired seeds compare uniform sampling,
$p(1-p)=u_2$, and $u_{16}$ under identical discounted Beta tracking. The
internally frozen primary is target-uniform normalized transition-AUC for
$u_{16}-p(1-p)$, requiring a positive exact two-sided sign-flip test at $.05$
and a point estimate of at least $+.01$.

\subsection{Does the deployed-$N$ peak location matter?}
\label{sec:peaktest}

The peak $p^\star_N$ falls $.500\to.169\to.064$ for $N=2,16,64$, so in every
study above the winning arm is also the harder-peaked arm: ``harder peaks beat
$p(1-p)$'' and ``the deployed-$N$ peak is correct'' predict identically there,
and only the second would advance on the $p(1-p)$ literature. Two preregistered
campaigns separate them, holding the estimator at $N=16$ in every arm and
varying only the sampling exponent, analyzed once each.

An over-shooting arm scored by $u_{64}$ does not lose to $u_{16}$: the paired
difference $u_{16}-u_{64}$ is $-.0113$ (95\% CI $[-.0297,+.0057]$, exact
$p=.235$, 10/20) on one platform and $-.0128$ ($[-.0284,+.0024]$, $p=.131$,
7/20) on a second. A seven-point exponent sweep
$N_{\text{score}}\in\{2,4,8,16,32,64,128\}$ gives arm means
$.644,.668,.665,.675,.679,.688,.681$, with argmax at $u_{64}$, not at the
deployed $u_{16}$, and Spearman rank correlation $+.93$ between exponent and
mean. \textbf{The curve rises past the deployed $N$: peak-location specificity
is not supported at this operating point.}

The same campaigns reproduce the shape effect on the shared seeds. Against
$p(1-p)$, $u_{16}$ wins
by $+.0322$ ($[+.0198,+.0449]$, exact $p=.000105$, 17/20) and $+.0307$
($[+.0166,+.0453]$, $p=.000507$, 16/20), versus $+.0480$ in V2 --- the first
reproductions outside the machine that produced V2, both with a smaller
$p$-value than V2's own. Magnitude is platform-sensitive (about $35\%$
attenuation on identical seeds between architectures), so we attribute
each number to its platform. Because the two new campaigns share seeds and a
deterministic engine, their agreement is a portability check, not additional
independent seeds.

\paragraph{Why the curve rises past the deployed $N$.}
Branching an exact-gradient pool to measure true continuation utility
$U_H(x)=J(\mathrm{Train}_H(\theta;x))-J(\theta)$ at $H=8$, $u_{16}$ ranks it
far better than $p(1-p)$ ($+.106$, 10 seeds, exact $p=.002$), while under RLOO
--- whose mass \emph{is} $2p(1-p)$ --- the two are indistinguishable
($+.0001$). But $U_H$ peaks near $p\approx.02$, below both $p^\star_{16}$ and
$p^\star_{64}$: activity ranks utility well and \emph{locates} it poorly.
The mechanism is horizon: activity is a one-update coefficient envelope,
whereas exact transfer-matrix utility compounds state changes over $H$ updates
and can favor harder tasks (App.~\ref{app:protocols}).
Local activity and downstream benefit are also reported to diverge across
domains under GRPO \citep{tac}. Mechanism on 36 synthetic tasks, not evidence
for a sampler.

\subsection{Where the score is starved: activity as a PLR priority on AMaze}
\label{sec:amaze}

The natural competitive test drops the activity priority into
\texttt{minimax}'s robust PLR \citep{plr,minimax} in place of MaxMC, running
the shipped maze configurations verbatim. Our development sweep --- seeds
disjoint from any confirmatory block, 5{,}000 of the 30{,}000 shipped updates,
five paired seeds per arm --- is negative, and the reason is informative.
Upstream robust PLR scores $.629$ and the no-curriculum control $.539$, with
every activity variant below the control: replacing MaxMC with
$\mathbb E[u_N(p)\mid\mathcal D]$ at the shipped $32{\times}1$ batch scores
$.500$ ($N{=}8$; $0/5$ seeds beat MaxMC, exact $p{=}.0625$) and $.551$
($N{=}16$).

The cause is information, not shape. MaxMC reads critic disagreement
$\max\text{-return}-V(s_t)$ at every timestep, with memory of the best return
ever achieved on that level, while at the shipped \texttt{n\_eval}${=}1$ a
success-rate score gets one terminal Bernoulli per level visit --- a
two-valued posterior, nearly all ties under rank prioritisation --- and
\texttt{max\_episode\_steps}${=}250$ against a $256$-step rollout forbids a
second episode. Gating MaxMC by activity rather than replacing it recovers
most of the gap ($.590$; $-.039$ to upstream, $1/5$) without clearing it. The AMaze student is PPO with
GAE and never forms binary rollout groups, so the faithful mapping is outside
this evidence.

\subsection{A coarser task unit: mean pass rate is insufficient}
\label{sec:mazescore}

Every positive above is at 640 or 4{,}810 parameters with exact or
near-exact gradients. We preregistered the same contrast at neural scale: a
1.26M-parameter transformer on procedural $17\times17$ mazes across 13
goal-distance levels, estimator fixed to practical MaxRL at the deployed
$N=32$, three samplers ($u_{32}$, $p(1-p)$, uniform) sharing one Beta
posterior, decay, floor, level set, and per-block SFT checkpoint. Forty-eight
paired blocks; endpoint mean pass@8 coverage over ten RL evaluations minus its
post-SFT value; support required Holm $p<.05$, a positive bootstrap lower
bound, \emph{and} at least $+.005$.

The primary is \textbf{practically ruled out} by the frozen rule: $u_{32}$
minus $p(1-p)$ is $-.00324$ (95\% CI $[-.00543,-.00111]$, exact $p=.0054$,
15/48), the interval's upper bound falling below the SESOI. The registered
secondary is supported ($u_{32}$ beats uniform by $+.00888$,
$[+.00657,+.01115]$, 41/48), so curriculum sampling helps here; what fails is
that the rollout-aware shape is the \emph{better} one. Per the branch fixed in
advance, effects at or above the SESOI are ruled out under this protocol and
no universal non-transfer is claimed.



\paragraph{Why: the curriculum names a coarser unit than the theory.}
The trainer draws one concrete maze per group and repeats that prompt $N$
times, while the teacher's posterior pools over a level's many mazes: the
i.i.d.\ unit is the maze, the scored unit is the level. Prop.~\ref{prop:mass}
still holds exactly --- what fails is its $p$-only reduction, which
Cor.~\ref{cor:gran} prices. The telemetry confirms both identities to floating
point across $41{,}101$ (seed, arm, level, window) cells, each to
$<5\times10^{-16}$ and unchanged at window widths $10$, $25$, $50$
(App.~\ref{app:protocols}). The gap is large where the score aims: at $\hat
p\approx.11$ the binomial predicts $2.2\%$ silent groups and $51.2\%$ are
silent (Fig.~\ref{fig:mazescore}). Clustered on the 48 seed blocks, $u_{32}$
realizes $.580$ $[.570,.590]$ of its prediction against $.703$ $[.691,.715]$
for $p(1-p)$ (paired $-.123$, 48/48 blocks negative), losing $60.8\%$ of its
groups to silence against $32.1\%$. Corollary~\ref{cor:gran} predicts this
activity-calibration gap, not either learning sign; the diagnosis is post-hoc.

\paragraph{Prospective correction.}
P0 then held the estimator, $N$, warmstart, task generator, budget, posterior,
decay, floor and 48 paired seeds fixed, varying only the score applied to that
posterior: the i.i.d.-at-the-mean plug-in versus realized count-law activity.
The delivered intervention (mean visit TV $.33597$; frozen threshold $.05$)
improves cov-AUC by $+.00666$ (95\% CI $[+.00441,+.00887]$, exact paired
$p=9.56\times10^{-7}$, 40/48 positive blocks), exceeding the preregistered
$+.005$ SESOI. The frozen verdict is \textbf{supported}: the correction is
causally relevant on this substrate. Arm means are $-.0043$ (plug-in) and
$+.0024$ (count law) relative to their common post-SFT coverage $.280$: the
plug-in arm's RL is net-negative for held-out coverage here, while the
correction flips the sign. This does not show that the algebra
predicted the learning sign, that the correction alone explains the earlier
contrast, or that either arm beats $p(1-p)$.

\paragraph{Secondary records.}
The maze factorial, $N$-sweep, paid-probe, creation-channel and Countdown
records remain in App.~\ref{app:protocols}; none supports named-method
superiority. Their measurement lesson is to retain raw per-task outcomes so
mean accuracy can be separated from standard $\passat{k}$.

\takeaway{\textbf{If you are deploying this.}
\emph{(i)} Score with $u_M$ for $M\ge$ your deployed $N$: the deployed $N$ is a
floor on the score exponent, not an optimum (\S\ref{sec:peaktest}).
\emph{(ii)} Score the unit your estimator consumes; if the curriculum names a
coarser bucket, score it by the realized count law $\hat q_z-\hat p_z$ rather
than by $u_N(\hat p_z)$. Both come free from group outcomes you already log;
the preregistered MAZE-SCORE correction confirms this on one substrate.
\emph{(iii)} Do not expect activity to replace a per-timestep regret signal; at
one Bernoulli per visit it is a gate on a richer signal, not a signal
(\S\ref{sec:amaze}).
\emph{(iv)} Treat relabeling as a separate creation channel; its appendix-only
gate record does not support a deployment prescription here
(App.~\ref{app:protocols}).
\emph{(v)} Log the dead-group fraction and realized-versus-predicted mass
beside $\passat{k}$: those two diagnosed \S\ref{sec:mazescore}.}

\section{Related work}

Objective-side work such as MaxRL \citep{maxrl} and RL2ML \citep{rl2ml} asks
which population or finite-rollout objective a group estimator implements,
holding the task distribution fixed. Learnability curricula --- ProCuRL
\citep{procurl}, SFL \citep{sfl}, LILO \citep{lilo} --- adapt that distribution
using success variability; advantage-bandit curricula SEC \citep{sec} and DUMP
\citep{dump} estimate activity empirically from observed advantages; TAC
\citep{tac} adds first-order cross-domain transferability under GRPO. We study
a complementary object: the finite-group activity geometry a deployed estimator
induces (Def.~\ref{def:geom}), derived from its algebra before any curriculum
hypothesis is tested. Superiority over the methods below is untested and not
claimed.

\paragraph{Concurrent estimator and selection analyses.}
Closed-form magnitudes for the standard-deviation-normalized branch peak at
$p=.5$ independent of $N$ \citep{groupstd,actorcurator}; practical-MaxRL mass
instead peaks as $\ln N/N$. LZE's LLM-scale study derives approximate
$p(1-p)G/n$ GRPO gradient variance under atomic i.i.d., fixed-baseline and
homogeneous-score assumptions \citep{lze}; SPEED-RL motivates intermediate
difficulty through estimator SNR, but its current arXiv record flags unresolved
experimental bugs, so we cite its theoretical framing only \citep{speedrl}.
We derive exact coefficient activity under a count law, not either quantity.
F-GRPO treats rare correct modes omitted within a group by changing its
weighting \citep{fgrpo}; we change task selection and expose coarse pooling.
MoPPS establishes streaming Beta/Thompson selection \citep{mopps}; posterior
machinery is precedent, while count-law sufficiency is our question.

\paragraph{Curricula and rollout allocation.}
Intermediate-difficulty sampling predates this work: ProCuRL's proximal score
reduces to $p(1-p)$ under its optimal-success assumption \citep{procurl}, and
LILO \citep{lilo} prioritizes binary reward variance. More tightly, SFL's
realized score $(k/N)(1-k/N)$ \citep{sfl} is exactly
$M_{\mathrm{RLOO}}(k)(N-1)/(2N)$ at fixed $N$: our framework recovers SFL as
the count-law curriculum for RLOO rather than replacing it as a heuristic.
When the sampled count $n$ varies, SFL's $n/(n+1)$ correction compounds
$\mathbb E[\hat p(1-\hat p)]=p(1-p)(n-1)/n$, leaving a level-dependent
$(n-1)/(n+1)$ factor. The surviving distinction is estimator-specific geometry
(MaxRL peaks at $k=1$, RLOO/SFL at the middle), coarse-unit pooling, variable
group size, and scoring cost. For practical MaxRL, $u_N=A_N/2$ is its
arbitrary-$N$ expected half-mass. VCRL \citep{vcrl} thresholds realized group variance; PLR \citep{plr} and
ALP-GMM \citep{alpgmm} use TD error, staleness or learning progress and so
react to forgetting where instantaneous mass cannot --- our AMaze arm is robust
PLR \citep{minimax} with only the replay priority replaced (\S\ref{sec:amaze})
--- and generator-based UED \citep{paired,accel} creates tasks rather than
pricing a fixed pool. Prompt selectors \citep{greso,dps}, per-prompt rollout
counts \citep{vip} and adaptive allocators \citep{hora,vigor,dars,reinforceada}
act after the task distribution is given; we choose that distribution before
generation at fixed $N$. HORA's posterior-integrated hit utility makes the move
Cor.~\ref{cor:gran} formalizes --- integrate a nonlinear rollout utility over
the relevant distribution rather than evaluate it at a mean --- but for
epistemic uncertainty about one prompt, where ours is heterogeneity inside a
scored aggregate. BBG \citep{yuan} and boundary-aware curricula \citep{cai}
target hit probability or boundary metrics, not coefficient mass. RL2ML
\citep{rl2ml} separates finite-rollout objectives, update scale, variance and
metric gain; its $T=N$ alignment retains $K=0$ via the direct estimator or a
score-baseline control variate, while our $T=N-1$ statement is for the deployed
hybrid that centers only when $K>0$.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Coefficient mass ignores score-gradient direction, parameter sharing,
optimizer state, transfer and non-i.i.d.\ rollouts: it is not a learning
guarantee. The $N$-sweep is co-designed, Digits is a contextual bandit,
Acrobot is one 640-parameter fixed pool with inherited seeds, and the maze
factorial uses one small transformer and family. Separate $\alpha=.05$
primaries imply no paper-wide error rate.

MAZE-SCORE bounds one family, architecture, $N$ and budget and does not isolate
scale; its post-hoc diagnosis prices calibration, not endpoint mediation. P0
isolates the score and confirms $+.00666$ there, but establishes neither
transfer nor superiority of either arm to $p(1-p)$. The PLR study is a
development negative, not named-baseline superiority; robotics and other
secondary records remain in App.~\ref{app:protocols}.

Registration timing and raw-data availability remain incomplete for Digits,
Acrobot and the maze records. App.~\ref{app:artifact} itemizes each gap and the
55-row compact registry; they limit auditing and preclude a broad neural claim.

\section{Conclusion}

Estimator activity is a functional of the success-count law. Where the
curriculum scores the estimator's own unit, the deployed-$N$ shape beat both
$u_2$ and uniform sampling, reproduced twice with the same seeds; three
preregistered follow-ups then bounded it. On the coarse-unit substrate where
the original contrast reverses, a preregistered intervention confirms that
replacing the mean-pass-rate plug-in by the count-law functional improves
learning. The estimator defines the coefficient map; the curriculum defines
the unit over which it is averaged, and the two do not commute.
Evaluate data selection with the estimator it ships on, report $\passat{k}$
from retained outcomes, and treat activity as a gate, not a law.

\paragraph{Truncation order.}
For $K>0$, conditioning on one sampled response and using exchangeability
gives
\[
\mathbb E\hat g
=\left(\frac{1-(1-p)^N}{p}-(1-p)^{N-1}\right)\nabla p.
\]
Since $1-(1-p)^N=p\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-p)^j$, subtracting the final term
leaves
$\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}(1-p)^j\nabla p=\nabla J_{N-1}$.
If the centered control-variate term is retained on $K=0$, its contribution is
$(1-p)^{N-1}\nabla p$. The expectation becomes
$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-p)^j\nabla p=\nabla J_N$. Thus the direct/non-dropped
convention has order $N$, while the deployed hybrid convention above has order
$N-1$; theorem and implementation must use the same convention.
At audited MaxRL source commit \texttt{7197bbb}, the released path computes
$(r_i-\bar r)/(\bar r+\epsilon)$ and zeros all-failure groups; after the
deployed gradient average, its $\epsilon\to0$ coefficients match this
convention up to common scale. The parsed truncation-order setting is inactive
on that path.

\paragraph{Coefficient mass.}
For $0<K<N$, each success has coefficient $(N-K)/(KN)$ and each failure
has coefficient $-1/N$. The two classes each contribute $(N-K)/N$ in
absolute value. For $K\in\{0,N\}$ the deployed centered estimator
contributes zero. With $K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(N,p)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_N(p)
&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)
  \binom{N}{k}p^k(1-p)^{N-k}\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\{\mathbb E[N-K]-N\Pr[K=0]\}\\
&=2\{1-p-(1-p)^N\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The peak follows from $u_N'(p)=N(1-p)^{N-1}-1$ and strict concavity for
$N>1$.

\paragraph{Per-member relabeling.}
For each mass in Def.~\ref{def:geom}, direct substitution gives
$M_{\mathcal E}(0)=M_{\mathcal E}(N)=0$. Per-member achieved-goal relabeling
makes every member successful under its own goal and hence maps $K=0$ to
$K'=N$, proving Lemma~\ref{lem:relabel}. A common group goal can instead yield
$1\le K'<N$ and positive mass, but that algebra alone implies no utility.

\paragraph{Posterior-integrated activity.}
For $p\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,b)$,
$\mathbb E[p]=a/(a+b)$ and the beta-function ratio gives
\[
\mathbb E[(1-p)^N]
=\frac{B(a,b+N)}{B(a,b)}
=\frac{(b)_N}{(a+b)_N}.
\]
Substitution in $u_N(p)=1-(1-p)^N-p$ proves the deterministic posterior
priority in \S\ref{sec:method}. Subtracting it from $u_N(\mathbb E[p])$
gives the printed nonnegative Jensen gap.

\paragraph{Estimator limits.}
Under the deployed $1/N$ gradient-average normalization, RLOO expected half-mass is
$p(1-p)=u_2(p)$, so $u_N(p)/u_2(p)\to N-1$ as $p\to0$. Near mastery,
both are first order in $q=1-p$. With sample-SD normalization, GRPO group
mass is proportional to $\mathbb E[\sqrt{K(N-K)}]$ up to deployed
normalization constants; its relative tail scaling is verified by exact
enumeration in \texttt{verify\_guidance\_math.json}. These are coefficient
statements, not gradient-direction statements.

\paragraph{Dependent rollouts.}
The per-group identity does not require independence. For any binary group,
\[
\mathbb E\sum_i|w_i|=2\{\Pr(K\ge1)-\mathbb E[K]/N\}.
\]
With a common marginal $p$, the second term is $p$; independence is used only
to replace $\Pr(K\ge1)$ by $1-(1-p)^N$. In a post-guidance exact
beta-binomial CPU stress
test, count-distribution enumeration matched the general identity to
$2.58\times10^{-13}$. At $\rho=.10$, the activity peak moved from $.169$ to
$.234$ for $N=16$ and from $.106$ to $.186$ for $N=32$. This dependence model
shows how increasing positive correlation can lower activity inside that
family; pairwise correlation alone does not determine a general joint law.
The result is a scope diagnostic, not evidence that coefficient mass causes
learning or that beta-binomial dependence describes neural rollouts.

\paragraph{Posterior-mean bias and the uncertainty penalty.}
With $p_x\mid\mathcal D\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a_x,b_x)$, strict concavity of
$u_N$ for $N>1$ and Jensen's inequality give
$\mathbb E[u_N(p_x)\mid\mathcal D]\leq u_N(\mathbb E[p_x\mid\mathcal D])$, so a
priority computed from the posterior mean overstates expected activity. The
gap is nonnegative and closed-form,
\[
u_N\!\left(\frac{a_x}{a_x+b_x}\right)-\mathbb E[u_N(p_x)\mid\mathcal D]
=\frac{(b_x)_N}{(a_x+b_x)_N}-\left(\frac{b_x}{a_x+b_x}\right)^N,
\]
and vanishes as the posterior concentrates. It is an uncertainty penalty, not
an exploration bonus: it shrinks the priority of tasks whose pass rate is
still poorly determined.

\paragraph{Posterior teacher.}
Each task stores discounted successful and failed rollout counts. A Thompson
draw $\tilde p_x$ is mapped through $u_N$ and mixed with a uniform floor.
Teacher state, sampler cursor, and checkpoints restore together. Observations
use pre-relabel rewards so recycled outcomes do not inflate the requested
task's posterior.

\paragraph{Coefficient-Activity PLR (development negative).}
The AMaze implementation is a narrow overlay on robust PLR in the pinned
\texttt{minimax} substrate \citep{plr,minimax}. It uses cumulative Beta
evidence per replay-buffer slot and replaces MaxMC priority with deterministic
$\mathbb E[u_N(p)\mid\mathcal D]$; the declared $N$ may equal
\texttt{n\_eval} (exact grouping) or be decoupled from it. Buffer admission,
the robust replay gate, inverse-rank mass, staleness mixing, and
with-replacement draws remain PLR mechanisms. Tie-aware ranking is shared with
the matched MaxMC control. \S\ref{sec:amaze} reports the development sweep:
the replacement priority does not beat upstream robust PLR, and no
baseline-performance claim is made.

A separate preregistered full-budget test kept MaxMC and gated it by activity.
Across ten paired seeds, gate minus upstream solved rate was $+.0633$ (95\% CI
$[+.0003,+.1410]$, exact sign-flip $p=.1562$, 5/10 positive). The frozen
verdict is \textsc{Inconclusive at n=10}: the point estimate cleared the
$+.02$ SESOI but not the p-value condition, while the interval did not trigger
the negative branch. This appendix-only result does not promote the gate claim
or alter the standalone-priority negative above.

\paragraph{Relabeling (secondary).}
For an all-fail request, a verified achieved goal can create an auxiliary
update where repeating the request would emit none. A parser proposes that
goal from a failed response, and the environment's
exact verifier must accept the response under that goal. Prompt conditioning
and explicit response goal declarations are rewritten; answer spans,
arithmetic, decimals, signed values, and unknown contexts are preserved. The
reported-run implementation is frozen, while a conservative release wrapper
and 26 CPU regression/property tests protect future runs. Relabeled groups
remain auxiliary selected-data updates; no importance ratio converts them
into fresh on-policy target samples. The intervention changes both update dose
and direction, and its achieved-goal distribution is biased toward outputs the
policy already reaches \citep{her,gcsl}.

\paragraph{The creation channel, and how to gate it.}
Where activity is zero no sampler can help, and relabeling a failed rollout as
a success of the goal it did reach is the only channel that adds signal
(Fig.~\ref{fig:regimes}A). Two results carry to practice. Gating relabels by an
\emph{estimated destination pass rate} rather than by observed frequency is
registered and confirmed: one probe rollout per step recovers $98\%$ of the
frequency-to-oracle gap (AUC $.798\to.877$ against an oracle $.879$; 10/10
seeds), and probe budgets of 1, 4 and 16 are indistinguishable --- P-PB1
confirmed, while its monotonicity sub-hypothesis P-PB2 is not supported
(1/10 seeds). Descriptively, how relabels are \emph{grouped} matters as much as
whether they happen: each relabeled row as its own $K{=}1$ group scores
$.952$, one destination per group $.881$, and a shared-$K$ coupled form
$.749$, against $.705$ with no recycling --- both orderings 10/10 seeds,
ten-seed AUC means on the fixed chain pool, exploratory.

\paragraph{Fixed-completion $N$-sweep (post-guidance, descriptive).}

\paragraph{Fixed-completion $N$-sweep.}
The environment contains three nested 12-level chains and ten actions.
Every cell runs exactly 51,200 completions, with arms for uniform,
$p(1-p)$, and $u_N$ sampling under the practical MaxRL estimator, no
hindsight, and eight paired seeds. Checkpoints share a completion clock. The
post-guidance analysis is descriptive.

On the sampled-group and optimizer-update clocks, the $u_{16}-p(1-p)$
contrasts remain descriptively positive ($+.05294$ and $+.05366$). The
$u_{16}$ arm receives 2.40 fewer groups and 3.42 fewer updates per million
transitions, while native-success transition-AUC is $.31085$, $.30521$ and
$.37615$ across arms. These secondary axes identify no causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Digits factorial (internally frozen negative).}
On a stored 1,077/360/360 train/development/test split of
\texttt{sklearn} Digits, a 4,810-parameter float64 policy recomputes each
example's exact correct-class probability before every update. We crossed
practical MaxRL and RLOO with uniform, $p(1-p)$, and $u_8$ sampling. Five-rate
development selected learning rate $.1$ for MaxRL, RLOO, and the common-rate
check. Across 24 fresh paired blocks, the internally frozen interaction
was $+.01589$ (paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[-.01686,+.04712]$, exact two-sided
sign-flip $p=.350$; 15/24 positive), so it was not supported despite passing
the treatment-delivery gate. MaxRL did favor $u_8$ over $p(1-p)$ by
$+.20842$ (CI $[+.16791,+.24744]$; 23/24), but RLOO's frozen prediction
reversed: $p(1-p)-u_8=-.17665$ (CI $[-.22042,-.13593]$; 0/24). Moreover,
the matched samplers were below uniform for both MaxRL ($-.11279$) and RLOO
($-.37581$). Because every selected rate equaled $.1$, the tuned and common
schedules have byte-identical ledgers and checkpoints and identical scientific
summary content after removing phase/authorization labels; they are identity
checks, not independent replications.
This exact-$p$ contextual bandit therefore supports neither the planned
interaction nor a universal curriculum advantage; it shows why expected
coefficient mass must remain an estimator-activity proxy rather than a
learning law. Its internally hashed local lock is not backed by an immutable
public pre-execution commit that independently establishes timing.

\paragraph{Acrobot V3 (historical).}
The frozen control uses official \texttt{Acrobot-v1}, eight nested thresholds
from $-1.5$ through native success at $1.0$, one shared H64 actor, practical MaxRL,
$N=16$, and no hindsight. Uniform and $u_{16}$ receive 20 paired seeds and a
nominal two million actual transitions per run, completing the final group.
Evaluation uses 32 shared trajectories total, reused across all eight
predicates, at initialization and each 100,000 transitions with fixed common
random numbers. The internally frozen primary is
target-uniform normalized transition-AUC; group diagnostics and evaluation
curves for all 40 runs are vendored. A post-hoc audit found cross-seed overlap
between actor-initialization and action-sampling RNG roots, so the retained
effect and mechanism audit are descriptive rather than clean confirmation.
The mechanism audit is deterministic from those records.

\paragraph{Acrobot fixed-pool tournament V2.}
The replacement reruns uniform and $u_{16}$ and adds $p(1-p)=u_2$ on fresh
logical seeds 20000--20019. All 60 source/runtime-locked runs completed after
an outcome-blind development gate; globally disjoint RNG roots and per-run
group-diagnostic and aggregate-checkpoint ledgers were retained. The primary
$u_{16}-p(1-p)$ transition-AUC difference is $+.04803$ (paired bootstrap 95\%
CI $[+.02094,+.07385]$, exact sign-flip $p=.003361$), clearing the
predeclared $+.01$ estimate filter; Table~\ref{tab:acrobot} gives the
Holm-controlled uniform comparisons. Observed mass means are close between
adaptive arms ($.7344$ versus $.7490$ per group; $115.57$ versus $115.90$
per million transitions), both descriptive paired intervals include zero,
and no equivalence test was frozen. The $p$-value tests zero rather than the
SESOI, and pairing does not guarantee sign exchangeability. The $p(1-p)$ arm
isolates only the score on our scaffold, not full ProCuRL or SFL.

\paragraph{Paid-probe ProCuRL selection semantics.}
This is a separate 80-pair family, not an extension of the V2 table. It keeps
the same eight thresholds, H64 actor, practical MaxRL estimator, $N=16$, and
$3\times10^{-4}$ learning rate. Its four arms are ordinary uniform; a uniform
sham with paid probes; source-faithful ProCuRL selection
$\operatorname{softmax}\{20\hat p(1-\hat p)\}$; and continuous-range-matched
$u_{16}$ selection. Probed arms run 20 episodes per task initially and at every
crossed 5,120-student-transition boundary; all student and probe transitions
count toward the nominal two-million paid budget. Across seeds 21000--21079,
the internally frozen fixed-paid-AUC contrast $u_{16}-$ProCuRL is
$+.004894$ (paired SD $.022139$, $t_{79}=1.9773$, $p=.05149$; 20,000-resample
CI $[+.000110,+.009727]$; one-million sign-flip $p=.05161$). It misses both
the $.02$ SESOI and the $.05$ paired-$t$ criterion. The five Holm-family
contrasts are ProCuRL--sham $-.001704$ (adjusted $p=.4568$),
$u_{16}$--sham $+.003190$ ($.4547$), ProCuRL--ordinary $-.313774$
($3.47\times10^{-66}$), $u_{16}$--ordinary $-.308880$
($9.48\times10^{-67}$), and sham--ordinary $-.312070$
($4.06\times10^{-68}$). Mean fixed-paid AUCs are $.33771$ (ProCuRL),
$.33942$ (sham), $.65149$ (ordinary), and $.34261$ ($u_{16}$).

The probed arms average 28.0--28.1 sweeps and 140.5--141.1k student
transitions versus 2.003M for ordinary uniform; 93.19--93.21\% of their paid
transitions are probes. They average 15.4--19.8 optimizer updates versus
259.1 ordinary-uniform updates, with mean paid-budget overshoot 69,960--72,939
transitions versus 2,984. All 320 confirmation and 12 outcome-blind
development runs reconcile exactly, all 11 development gates pass, and the
common-random-number invariants replay.

An earlier outcome-blind development wave was invalidated before any gate or
arm contrast because 73 entropy-sum replays differed by at most
$7.28\times10^{-12}$. Its seeds were burned and its incident receipt is
retained; the reported family uses fresh seeds and a replacement lock.

\paragraph{Post-guidance rollout-allocation factorial.}
We also compared fixed $N=16$, published-HORA-style posterior hit allocation
\citep{hora}, and a mass-aware marginal that counts four already-spent probe
rollouts. A $2$-sampler by $3$-allocator CPU factorial uses 16 paired seeds and
51,200 completions per cell. The mass-aware arm improves $\passat{8}$ AUC over
HORA-style allocation by $+.00676$ (11/16 positive pairs) and over fixed groups
by $+.02517$ (14/16). However, it \emph{reduces} realized coefficient mass per
completion by $.000306$ relative to HORA-style allocation in all 16 pairs,
falsifying the frozen mechanism prediction. Its group sizes are also extreme
(cell-average maximum about 76 versus 57 for HORA and 16 fixed). We therefore
treat the result as exploratory motivation for posterior-calibration and
allocation-cap ablations, not a fourth contribution.

A frozen follow-up crossed two samplers, the two adaptive scores, four caps,
and three information sources over 16 paired seeds (50 cells; 800 runs).
Restricting to the 32 deployable-information adaptive cells, all 32
adaptive-minus-fixed mean $\passat{8}$-AUC contrasts were positive, with 22
unadjusted descriptive Student-$t$ intervals above zero; no
multiplicity-adjusted inference is assigned. Yet realized coefficient mass
was lower in 28/32 cells, with 24 intervals below zero and none above zero.
The remaining 16 oracle-preupdate cells are diagnostic. Thus the expanded
result reinforces the learning--mass mismatch rather than a mediation claim.
The frozen engineering rule chose cap 32: averaged
over samplers for the fresh-group/same-step cells, it reduced maximum group
size by 58.07\% with an AUC change of $-.00271$ versus uncapped. This is a
point-estimate filter for a future pilot, not an equivalence test. The
source/runtime lock is internally hashed but lacks a public pre-execution
commit establishing timing.

\paragraph{Maze factorials.}
Wave 1 has six shared seed/warm-start blocks and six arms per block: MaxRL and
GRPO under uniform and FrontierMax sampling plus two diagnostic GRPO arms.
Its recorded endpoint hypothesis failed. Wave 2 has six fresh blocks and
the four core arms. The external execution record names time-integrated $\passat{8}$
relative to warm start primary, with at least 5/6 positive blocks required
under each sampler. The block analyzer reports sampler-specific exact sign
tests, sampler-averaged contrasts, post-hoc intervals, and
leave-one-block-out checks. No pooled $p$-value treats sampler observations

The frozen multiverse covers integration rule, warmup inclusion, horizons,
and leave-one-checkpoint-out variants, but all 24 wave-2 checkpoint JSONLs and
warm starts remain external. Structured summaries recover the anchors
(uniform 6/6, mean $+.01496$; FrontierMax 6/6, $+.02404$;

\paragraph{Countdown.}
We fine-tune SmolLM2-360M with $N=16$. The v2 pool uses operand permutation,
parenthesization, and exact division.
Tier 1 has 128 evaluation tasks and 16 verifier-scored samples per task. B1
disables recycling; B2 admits exact-verifier achieved goals; the committed
scoreboard contains three-seed aggregates. ARM B disables recycling and uses
two optimizer epochs on every live group; its three individual endpoints are
vendored. Headline endpoints are step-60 mean@16 and VERL bootstrap best@16.
The latter uses 1,000 with-replacement resamples of size 16 per prompt and is not standard
unbiased $\passat{16}$; missing per-task outcomes prevent recomputation. A
stored task-identity summary reports zero tier-1 and tier-2 SFT overlap and
27 exposed tier-0 tasks; the source manifests needed to recompute those
counts are absent. Tier 0 is excluded from absolute claims.

The reported recycling-package aggregate moves tier-1 mean@16 from
$.278\pm.066$ to $.324\pm.014$ while bootstrap best@16 moves from
$.541\pm.024$ to $.492\pm.013$ (mean $\pm$ sample SD across three seeds).
Complete three-seed B1/B2 records are not vendored, so this supports neither a
paired seed test nor a named-method effect claim. It is retained only as a
measurement caution: mean accuracy alone cannot reveal whether coverage was
spent to buy it.
